# Learning to Learn

Embed learning strategies and metacognition as a **parallel goal** within a concept-centered lesson. Keep the subject concepts central while changing how learners experience, check, and consolidate them.

`stage-design` still governs creation and persistence. When using `/understanding-by-design`, establish big ideas, essential questions, and performance tasks before embedding strategies. When also using `/social-emotional-learning`, both parallel goals must serve the same conceptual task rather than create separate course storylines.

## Declare parallel goals

Before planning pages, declare separately:

1. **Concept goal:** what learners should understand, explain, or transfer.
2. **Learning-to-learn goal:** the learning action to practice and the observable behavior that demonstrates it occurred.

Every strategy must explain which conceptual understanding it serves. Remove any embedding that cannot. Prefer the one or two strategies most relevant to the task; do not list an entire vocabulary of learning science in one lesson.

## Two content boundaries

1. **Planning labels stay off student pages.** Internal terms such as `learning seam`, `metacognition`, `instructional intention`, and `parallel goal` belong in planning and briefs, rather than student-facing titles, body text, or section labels.
2. **Role dialogue belongs in narration.** Put teacher and student-agent speech, demonstrations, and discussion in narration/actions. Static pages carry concepts, questions, tasks, and cues, rather than "Teacher says" or "Student says" lines.

Use immediately actionable wording instead of technical terms:

- Retrieval practice: "Before looking it up, write your answer."
- Self-explanation: "Give a reason for the conclusion in your own words."
- Prediction before verification: "Make a prediction, then check it."
- Spaced review: "Return in a few days and recall it without looking."
- Understanding monitoring: "Do you understand it, or does it only feel familiar?"

Use a strategy's technical term as learning content only when the user explicitly asks to teach that method itself.

## Four embedding points

These are planning anchors, rather than page titles or a requirement for four additional pages:

1. **Opening:** before instruction, retrieve prior knowledge, make an initial judgment, or record a prediction.
2. **Concept building:** after reaching a conclusion, explain it in your own words and ask why. A student agent may model an incomplete explanation and invite the learner to add to or challenge it.
3. **Doing:** in `interactive`, require prediction or an answer before feedback. Errors must lead to checking, repair, and retry rather than a dead end.
4. **Closing:** collect what I understood, how I learned it, what remains uncertain, and how I will retrieve it again later.

## Multi-agent roles

- **Teacher:** model thinking before answering, checking understanding, and locating gaps. Accept being stuck and guide repair.
- **Student agent:** present feeling familiar without being able to recall, an incorrect prediction, or an incomplete explanation. Invite the learner to judge, correct, and transfer.

Discuss methods and understanding, rather than judging people. The teacher returns the conversation to the concept.

## Quality gates

- Declare the concept goal and learning-to-learn goal separately.
- Identify the concept and observable action served by each embedding point.
- Learners actually perform at least one retrieval, self-explanation, or prediction-before-verification activity, rather than only hear about methods.
- Preserve feedback, repair, and retry after errors or frustration.
- Closing includes conceptual understanding, reflection on the learning process, and a later review action.
- Inspect every page's static text for internal planning labels and role dialogue.
- Do not promise a universal learning method. Verify sources before comparing research evidence.

## Boundaries

This skill embeds strategies into subject-concept lessons. It is not for a standalone course whose central subject is study methods or learning science. If the user wants a second full course storyline about learning methods, confirm the scope with `ask_user` first.
