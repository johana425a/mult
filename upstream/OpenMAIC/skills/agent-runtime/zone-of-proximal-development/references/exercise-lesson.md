# Organizing a complete assessment-and-practice lesson

Use this guide to create or improve a lesson centered on applying prior knowledge, correcting difficulties, and checking independent performance. It is more than questions and scores, and does not reteach a whole knowledge unit from the beginning.

## Organize by task relationships, rather than quantity

Establish one core goal and observable success criteria, then allocate time. A short lesson of about 20 minutes can contain six functions below. Adjust page count to content and platform capabilities.

| Part | Purpose and learner action | Design requirements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Goals and rules (about 1 minute) | Know what to practice and what evidence to retain | Explain independent work before help, and reasons as well as scores. Do not reveal the diagnostic solution at opening. |
| Independent diagnosis (about 3 minutes) | Submit the answer and key reason currently possible | Use a few discriminating tasks, without hints or answering for the learner. Do not prefill error causes before a response. |
| Targeted review (about 4 minutes) | Compare actual work, distinguish a key error, and understand the method | Explain why the method works. Label typical errors as examples. A page of solutions is not a diagnosis of a real learner. |
| Differentiated practice (about 5 minutes) | Obtain help as needed and personally complete the key action | Offer prerequisites or step cards for difficulty. Already-capable learners can skip guidance or use comparable variants. State who selects the route. |
| Independent check on a fresh task (about 5 minutes) | Complete a new task without hints, with reasons or a check | Match the lesson goal and avoid introducing new rules simultaneously. Check before explaining this task. |
| Closure and next steps (about 2 minutes) | Explain what was independent, what help was used, and choose a next step | Report change only with evidence. Return remaining difficulty to a specific obstacle. Offer meaningful later practice without declaring permanent mastery. |

These times are design estimates, rather than measured learning time. A teacher-led lesson can adapt review after receiving actual answers. A classroom generated in one pass should present review, demonstrations, and routes as selectable materials rather than inventing real-time adaptation.

## Connect task sets and review

Assign each item's purpose: diagnosis, review example, supported practice, independent check, or extension. Do not add every item into one homogeneous score. Tasks sharing key transformations or reasoning but changing surface data suit an initial fading check. Tasks requiring new knowledge are extensions rather than evidence of established independence.

Review at least one key relationship for the goal, rather than listing answers. You can organize it as "What is wrong? Why does it fail? How can it be repaired? Check your own work," while preserving the learner's opportunity to distinguish the error. Check stems, choices, narration, and AI-peer speech for answer leakage across demonstration, supported, and independent items.

For positive-integer `ax+b=c`, diagnosis could use `3x+5=20`; review could use another equation to compare changing one side with performing the same operation on both; supported practice could use `4x+3=19`; and the independent check could use `5x+4=34`. Do not explain `5x+4=34` before the check or suddenly introduce fractions or negative coefficients. Other subjects should use their own key actions rather than copying this equation template.

## Provide executable practice routes

A static lesson needs at least two usable choices:

- Independent success with sufficient reasons: skip step-by-step guidance and go to a fresh check. Return to review or help if that task becomes difficult.
- A specific difficulty: go to matching review or supported practice. Use a smaller prerequisite task if needed instead of repeatedly increasing difficulty.

State real navigation instructions, such as opening the page list and selecting the named title. Do not draw an unimplemented automatic-routing or mastery-unlock button. Page turning alone does not demonstrate fading. Update route instructions after reordering pages or questions.

If thumbnails reveal review content, remind learners to collapse the page list and help during independent work. Do not describe a freely navigable lesson as a technically enforced exam without hints.

## Records and closure

Ask learners to review actual responses and retain a short "initial answer - help - fresh task - next step" record. Label help use reported by the learner as self-report. Without automatic cross-page aggregation, let the learner fill it in or take it back to the workbench rather than inventing an automatically generated learning report.

`quiz` response records and temporary input in generated `interactive` pages may use different persistence mechanisms. Prefer components whose submission and later retrieval have been verified when exit-safe records are required. If an interactive page only preserves state within the current document, remind learners to retain it elsewhere. A higher retake score is not the first independent performance.

## Whole-lesson acceptance

Read the actual saved lesson and establish: its goal; which needs diagnosis identifies; why review addresses them; where learners still act personally; who selects help or skipping; whether the fresh check removes key hints; and how observed performance determines next steps.

Walk both a help-needed route and a skip-guidance route. Confirm that answers do not leak before independent submission, navigation is correct, help works, and required responses can be retrieved. Report content persistence, functional checks, audio, and student learning outcomes separately; one does not establish the others.
