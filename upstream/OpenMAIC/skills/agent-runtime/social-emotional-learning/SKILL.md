# Social-Emotional Learning (SEL)

Embed social-emotional learning as a **parallel goal** within a concept-centered lesson. Preserve the subject concept the user wants to learn while changing how learners experience it.

`stage-design` still governs creation and persistence. When using `/understanding-by-design`, establish big ideas, essential questions, and performance tasks before embedding SEL in page plans. Every SEL design must explain which conceptual understanding it serves; remove designs that cannot.

## Declare parallel goals

Before planning pages, separately state in one sentence each:

1. **Concept goal:** what learners should understand or transfer.
2. **SEL parallel goal:** the capacity to practice during this learning and the observable behavior showing it occurred.

Choose the one or two capacities most relevant to the conceptual task, rather than trying to cover all of them in one classroom:

- **Self-awareness and self-management:** notice confusion, frustration, curiosity, or distraction and take one regulatory step.
- **Social awareness and empathy:** reconsider the same content from another person's or stakeholder's perspective.
- **Relationship skills and collaboration:** explain reasoning, receive and respond to constructive criticism, and challenge ideas rather than people.
- **Responsible decision-making:** evaluate the consequences of explanations or actions and justify a choice.

## Four SEL embedding points

Do not add four pages merely for SEL. Embed the goals in existing page briefs and interactions:

1. **Opening hook:** add a short awareness moment to the opening `slide`, inviting learners to name how they feel about where they might get stuck.
2. **Concept building:** design a substantive multi-agent disagreement on a `slide` to practice perspective-taking and collaboration. Differences concern ideas rather than people.
3. **Doing:** give `interactive` an expected frustration, feedback, and retry path that practices self-management and resilience. Failure must not be a dead end.
4. **Closing reflection:** ask which concept the learner understood and which social-emotional capacity they practiced; express the two gains separately.

## Multi-agent roles

- **Teacher:** help learners name feelings, take other perspectives, acknowledge reasonable disagreement, and return discussion to the concept.
- **Student agent:** offer different understandings, sincere questions, or a stuck moment, and invite the learner to participate, judge, or help revise.

Do not make agents recite SEL terminology. Let the capacity appear through observable dialogue, choices, feedback, and retries.

## Quality and safety gates

- Declare SEL and concept goals separately, rather than combining them into a vague promise of comprehensive competence.
- Identify the concept and observable SEL capacity served by every embedding point.
- Accept emotional expression without judgment. Do not require public disclosure of trauma, mental-health conditions, or private experiences.
- Do not diagnose psychological conditions, pathologize learning frustration, or drive participation through humiliation, public rankings, or peer pressure.
- `interactive` must offer a frustration-feedback-retry path. The closing page presents conceptual gains and capacity gains.

## Boundaries

This skill embeds SEL into subject-concept lessons. It is not for standalone counseling, advisory, psychology, homeroom, or emotion lessons whose central subject is SEL. If the user wants SEL as a second full storyline alongside the subject, confirm the scope with `ask_user` first.
